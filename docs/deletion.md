# Staging and permanent deletion

Space or `d` keeps a selection in an in-memory basket without a filesystem walk, move or deletion. Press `D` to check the whole basket and prepare a fresh review. Folder plans include **all folder contents, including hidden files and items excluded from the scan**. Review the full selected paths, fresh object counts and allocation; inspect the manifest when needed. Allocation is not an estimate of space that deletion will reclaim.

The final in-app review requires the exact phrase `DELETE <selected-target-count>` and Enter. Confirmation applies only to that prepared plan. Filtering and navigation never act as confirmation. Read-only, imported, incomplete and error results have no deletion capability. Refresh from a live root before preparing a new plan. Cancelling review discards the prepared authority; the observational basket can be checked again.

Permanent deletion currently supports local writable APFS with ownership enabled. It requires a trusted same-filesystem quarantine anchor outside the selection. Ordinary user-owned directories with protected mode/ACL permissions are supported. Files in shared writable or unprovable source folders are retained because a safe metadata handle cannot be obtained there. A narrowly verified sticky-directory/current-user-owned-object case is allowed. Special nodes and crossed mounts are refused; there is no copy-and-delete fallback. Root/setuid deletion is disabled.

After confirmation, RDU creates a private recovery directory beneath the scan root. A flushed journal records the complete intent before any moves. Every object is captured without overwrite into its own protected slot, checked against a live handle and then permanently removed. Each directory receives a nonrecursive rmdir. Concurrent replacement files and newly inserted children are preserved; a nonempty directory can be restored or retained for recovery. Nothing is moved to Trash.

Cancellation while the initial intent journal is being written stops between bounded records and prevents every capture. RDU retains the verified owned recovery directory and its partial cancellation journal; all selected objects are reported as not attempted. Journal-record write or flush failure remains an error and does not become a successful cancellation. Records are limited to64KiB; an oversized record is rejected before its append.

Preparation supports cooperative cancellation and expires ten minutes after it finishes. Plans are limited to one million graph objects, depth1024 and approximately256MiB of manifest allocation accounting; retained scan data and other process memory are separate. Cancel during execution stops further admission and checks again before purge. A syscall already running can finish, and earlier permanent removals cannot be undone.

Read the result totals: **purged**, **preserved/restored**, **not attempted** and **uncertain** have different meanings. An operation may partially complete. Details are capped at4096 records/about8MiB; complete aggregate counts remain available, and the journal is retained when details are clipped. An append/flush failure means the journal may be incomplete. RDU reports the recovery location and stops rather than claiming a complete durable audit.

Keep recovery storage when an error, conflict or interruption occurs. Its raw-name journal is a reconciliation hint, not permission to delete or replay actions. RDU does not automatically purge or resume a journal after restart. Inspect live identities and existing destination names before deciding how to restore retained objects; never overwrite a re-created source name. Unexpected objects prevent automatic cleanup of the recovery directory. Recovery journaling itself consumes disk space.

This is permanent unlink/rmdir, not secure erasure, an atomic recursive transaction, a fixed-space-reclamation promise or complete crash rollback. Same-UID adversaries, root and untrusted kernels/filesystem drivers are outside the isolation boundary. Open writers can still change a confirmed inode. Inode reuse before a live pin and concurrent ancestor renames have documented limits. See [the implementation contract](next/deletion-service.md) and [security policy](../SECURITY.md).

Validation used disposable fixtures on the current APFS system. Foreign-account writers, actual ownership-disabled volumes, privileged mount transitions and arbitrary power-loss recovery were not exercised. Synthetic mount flags and device-shaped metadata are refusal-policy tests; no device was opened to test deletion.
